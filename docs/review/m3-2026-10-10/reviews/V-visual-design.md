# Reviewer V: visual design (top-studio lens)

## 1. Root causes

1. **Everything is in a box.** Every block of text sits in a rounded slab of #1B2D46 (navy-900, a whitened grey-navy, about 1.34:1 against the page), and boxes nest up to three deep: card, then tile, then pill. Containers stand in for composition, and that is why it looks like MS Paint.
2. **There is no grid and no spacing system.** Left edges fall at 128, 140, 163, 170, 282 and 316px. Widths come from viewport arithmetic (`50vw − 330px`). Spacing values include 6, 10, 14, 18, 22, 26 and 30px, so nothing lines up.
3. **The type is flat and the metadata is loud.** The page uses 11 sizes, and the 70px H1 is only 1.17× the 60px section heads. Five competing uppercase label styles, mostly mono in outlined pills, make the metadata as loud as the content.
4. **The 3D looks like unlit clip art and never meets the type.** The cubes are flat-shaded, #EDEDED is the brightest value on the page, the floors have hard edges and stray arcs float around. The scene sits on the right while the text sits in boxes on the left.
5. **The product screens are pasted in, not art-directed.** There are letterboxed white JPEGs, `cover` crops that cut words in half, a gloss overlay and 5px text. The strongest evidence on the page looks the cheapest.

## 3. Scorecard

## 4. Would a top studio ship this?

No. As it stands it would go out as a wireframe with a placeholder 3D scene: the layout is built from boxes, pills and mono labels instead of a grid, a type scale and art-directed imagery. Before a client sees it, it needs a full second pass: remove every container, lock the 12-column grid and the 8pt rhythm, relight the scene and re-crop every product screen.
